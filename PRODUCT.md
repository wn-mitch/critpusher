# Critpusher

## Register

product

## Users

Players trying a physical coin-pusher toy in desktop and mobile browsers. Pointer, keyboard, and touch should support continuous placement and timing without camera management.

## Product Purpose

Test the rhythm of reading a shared physical pile, placing known upcoming coins, and resolving damage, healing, or currency from real catches and bomb detonations. The first-fight implementation has automated runtime proof; human readability, satisfaction, and combat balance remain acceptance gates.

## Roadmap

- **MVP 1 (shipped):** physical machine, dense pile, moving pusher, collection edge, opening rain, live tuning, measurement harness.
- **MVP 1.5 (shipped):** atomic coin-by-coin player/enemy drop sets and captured chute settings.
- **MVP 2 (acceptance pending):** six coin identities, visible shuffled feeds, batch HP combat, physical impact bombs, and seeded policy comparisons.
- **MVP 3:** progression, escalating pressure, persistence.

`DESIGN.md` holds coin effects, encounter lifecycle, and progression boundaries;
`TUNING.md` holds the measured record and study protocol.

## Brand Personality

Tactile, mechanical, playful. A painted arcade cabinet and metallic coins anchor the presentation. Whirrs, blips, clinks, and payout dings are core feedback, not decorative polish.

## Anti-references

Avoid a dashboard surrounding a tiny game, casino marketing, neon overload, pachinko, and visual ornament that obscures the pile.

## Design Principles

- Keep the physical machine dominant and the collection edge visible.
- Preserve continuous dropping rhythm and readable consequences.
- Use actual stacking and contacts, not an animation pretending to simulate a pile.
- Aggregate avalanche feedback without losing logical payouts.
- Treat timing, density, and satisfaction as measured or playtested properties, not assertions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Offer reduced decorative motion independently of sound. Provide mute and volume, keyboard and touch controls, large targets, visible focus, non-color feedback, a fixed camera, and readable portrait/landscape layouts. Audio is an important part of play but is not the only payout signal.
